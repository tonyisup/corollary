# M4 Balanced Scorer Dataset — Results

> **Date:** 2026-05-14
> **Status:** Documented, not closing the gap

## Goal

Test whether a balanced training dataset (equal self and other captures) can close the fixed-threshold scorer gap that the per-speaker adaptive threshold was designed to solve.

## Approach

Created two balanced training datasets from existing captures:
- **v1**: 6 self (1 real + 5 synthetic) + 6 other captures, aug_factor=5
- **v2**: 6 self (1 real + 5 synthetic with more variety) + 6 other captures, aug_factor=5

Synthetic self captures were generated by playing TTS-like signals through the estimated room IR.

## Results

| Scorer | Aggregate F1 | Eligible F1 | Self Recall | Neg FP |
|--------|-------------|-------------|-------------|--------|
| feature2 (baseline) | 0.7797 | 0.7979 | 0.9390 | 62 |
| balanced_v1 | 0.8512 | 0.8786 | 0.7500 | 2 |
| balanced_v2 | 0.7619 | 0.7805 | 0.8780 | 65 |

**Acceptance criteria**: Eligible F1 ≥ 0.95 AND Neg FP = 0

None of the three scorers pass acceptance.

## Analysis

The balanced training creates a precision-recall tradeoff:
- **v1** (conservative): Great neg FP (2) but poor self-recall (0.75) — the synthetic self captures don't match real self audio well enough
- **v2** (more variety): Better self-recall (0.878) but terrible neg FP (65) — the varied synthetic data confuses the decision boundary
- **baseline** (imbalanced): Good self-recall (0.939) but terrible neg FP (62) — the 8:1 class imbalance biases toward "other"

## Conclusion

A single global fixed-threshold scorer cannot simultaneously achieve 0 FP and high self-recall on room-colored live audio. The domain gap between synthetic training data and live audio is too large.

**The viable path remains**: per-speaker adaptive threshold (M4+) + conversational registration. The global scorer should only be revisited with a deliberately balanced live dataset collected across multiple held-out sessions with real human speech — not synthetic data.
